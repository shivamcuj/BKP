Metabolism of Pyrimidine Nucleotides

"Study with love, remember with ease."

Part I: Comprehensive Guide

Introduction

The primary pyrimidine nucleotides are essential building blocks for DNA and RNA:

  • CMP: Cytidine monophosphate
  • UMP: Uridine monophosphate
  • TMP: Thymidine monophosphate
Key Concept: Unlike purines (where the ring is built directly on the sugar), the six-membered pyrimidine ring is synthesized first and then attached to the ribose phosphate, which is donated by PRPP.

Ring Precursors

De novo synthesis requires three fundamental precursors to form the pyrimidine ring:

  • N3 Provided by Glutamine
  • C4, C5, C6, N1 Furnished by Aspartic Acid
  • C2 Provided by Carbon Dioxide (CO2)

The 12 Major Steps of De Novo Synthesis

Carbamoyl Phosphate Formation

Glutamine + ATP + CO2 → Carbamoyl phosphate. Catalyzed by cytosolic Carbamoyl phosphate synthase-II (CPS-II).
(Note: CPS-I is mitochondrial and used for the urea cycle).

The Committed Step

Carbamoyl phosphate condenses with Aspartate to form Carbamoyl aspartate. Catalyzed by Aspartate transcarbamoylase. This is the main regulatory committed step!

Ring Closure

Removal of water (H2O) from carbamoyl aspartate by Dihydro-orotase closes the ring, forming Dihydro-orotic acid.

Oxidation to Orotate

Dihydro-orotic acid is oxidized to Orotic acid by Dihydro-orotate dehydrogenase.
(Unique fact: This is the ONLY mitochondrial enzyme in this pathway; the rest are cytosolic!)

Acquisition of Ribose Phosphate

Orotate + PRPP → Orotidine monophosphate (OMP). Catalyzed by Orotate phosphoribosyl transferase.

Formation of UMP

OMP undergoes decarboxylation (loss of CO2) to yield Uridylate (UMP). Enzyme: Orotidylate decarboxylase.

Phosphorylation to UDP, UTP & CTP

  • Step 7: UMP + ATP → UDP (Catalyzed by Kinase)
  • Step 8: UDP + ATP → UTP (Catalyzed by Kinase)
  • Step 9: UTP + Glutamine → CTP (Catalyzed by CTP synthase, amination step)

Deoxyribonucleotide & dTMP Synthesis

  • Step 10: UDP → dUDP (by Ribonucleotide reductase, requires NADPH).
  • Step 11: dUDP is dephosphorylated to dUMP.
  • Step 12: dUMP is methylated by N5,N10-methylene THF to form dTMP (Catalyzed by Thymidylate synthase).

Regulation of De Novo Synthesis

Regulation primarily occurs via allosteric control of the first two enzymes:

1. Carbamoyl Phosphate Synthase-II (CPS-II)

Inhibited by: UTP Activated by: PRPP

2. Aspartate Transcarbamoylase

Inhibited by: CTP Activated by: ATP

Catabolism of Pyrimidines

Unlike purines (which degrade to sparingly soluble uric acid), pyrimidines degrade into highly water-soluble products.

End Products:

  • CO2
  • NH3
  • β-alanine
  • β-aminoisobutyrate

Metabolic Fates:

  • β-alanine: Serves as a precursor for acetyl-CoA.
  • β-aminoisobutyrate: Transaminated to methylmalonate semialdehyde → methylmalonyl-CoA → succinyl-CoA.

Part II: Last-Minute Exam Revision

1. The Basics

  • Rule: Ring is made FIRST, then ribose (PRPP) is added.
  • Precursors: Glutamine (N3), Aspartic Acid (C4,C5,C6,N1), CO2 (C2).

2. Vital Enzymes

  • CPS-II: First step (cytosolic). Forms Carbamoyl phosphate.
  • Aspartate transcarbamoylase: The Committed Step.
  • Dihydro-orotate dehydrogenase: The ONLY mitochondrial enzyme in the pathway.
  • Thymidylate synthase: Converts dUMP → dTMP using N5,N10-methylene THF.

3. Regulation Cheat Sheet

CPS-II:
(+) PRPP   |   (-) UTP

Aspartate Transcarbamoylase:
(+) ATP   |   (-) CTP

4. Catabolism Outcomes

  • End products are highly water-soluble.
  • Cytosine/Uracil → β-alanine → Acetyl-CoA.
  • Thymine → β-aminoisobutyrate → Succinyl-CoA.
  • Also produces CO2 and NH3.
