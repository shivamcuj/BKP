Introduction to Nucleic Acid Metabolism

Nucleotides are the building blocks of life. Deoxyribonucleotides are crafted for DNA synthesis, while ribonucleotides breathe life into RNA synthesis.

The biosynthesis of purine and pyrimidine nucleotides is absolutely essential for DNA replication, cell division, and the growth of all mammalian cells, bacteria, and viruses.

💡 Example to Clarify:

If the supply of nucleotides is blocked, cell division halts. This is why many anti-tumor, anti-bacterial, and anti-viral agents function as inhibitors of nucleotide biosynthesis. By starving a cancer cell of purines, we stop it from multiplying!

Remarkably, purines and pyrimidines are dietary nonessential. Our bodies don't rely on the food we eat to build endogenous nucleic acids; instead, humans synthesize them de novo (from scratch) using amphibolic intermediates.

De Novo Biosynthesis Pathway

The two primary purine nucleotides are AMP (Adenosine monophosphate) and GMP (Guanosine monophosphate). In the de novo pathway, the purine ring is meticulously assembled directly onto a ribose-5-phosphate foundation.

The Molecular Contributors (Precursors)
  • Glycine Provides carbon-4 (C4), C5, and nitrogen-7 (N7).
  • Aspartate Provides nitrogen-1 (N1).
  • Glutamine Provides nitrogen-3 (N3) and nitrogen-9 (N9).
  • THF & CO₂ Tetrahydrofolate derivatives furnish C2 and C8. Respiratory CO₂ provides C6.
Major Steps to IMP, AMP, & GMP

1. Initiation: Ribose-5-phosphate (from the pentose phosphate pathway) is converted to PRPP (phospho-ribosyl-pyrophosphate) by PRPP synthetase using ATP.

2. The Committed Step: PRPP receives an amide group from Glutamine to form 5-phosphoribosylamine. Catalyzed by PRPP-amidotransferase. This is the rate-limiting step!

3-11. Building the Ring: Through a series of additions involving Glycine, THF, CO₂, and Aspartate, the structure eventually undergoes ring closure via IMP cyclohydrolase to form Inosine Monophosphate (IMP), the parent purine nucleotide.

12-15. Branching out:
- IMP + Aspartate + GTP → Adenylosuccinate → AMP
- IMP + NAD⁺ → XMP + Glutamine + ATP → GMP

Feedback Regulation

The body elegantly ensures it doesn't overproduce purines through feedback inhibition:

  • PRPP Synthetase: Inhibited by AMP and GMP.
  • PRPP-amidotransferase: Inhibited by AMP and GMP.
  • Branchpoints: AMP inhibits adenylosuccinate synthase; GMP inhibits IMP dehydrogenase.

Salvage Pathway

Nature's recycling program! Free purine bases formed during nucleic acid degradation are "salvaged" and attached to PRPP to form nucleotides, saving immense cellular energy.

Two key enzymes:

  • APRTase: Adenine + PRPP → AMP
  • HGPRTase: Hypoxanthine + PRPP → IMP
    Guanine + PRPP → GMP

Catabolism to Uric Acid

In humans, purines are ultimately degraded down to Uric Acid, which is sparingly soluble.

Dephosphorylation: AMP & GMP lose phosphate via nucleotidase to become Adenosine and Guanosine.

Deamination & Cleavage: Adenosine is deaminated to inosine (via adenosine deaminase). Inosine and Guanosine are cleaved to their free bases (Hypoxanthine and Guanine) via nucleoside phosphorylase.

Final Oxidation: Hypoxanthine → Xanthine → Uric Acid. Guanine → Xanthine → Uric Acid. The superstar enzyme here is Xanthine Oxidase.

Conceptual Example: Because uric acid is sparingly soluble, an overproduction or under-excretion of it leads to its crystallization in joints—a painful condition widely known as Gout.

Purine Metabolism Cheat Sheet

Quick recall for exams

1. The Basics

  • Need: DNA (deoxyribonucleotides) & RNA (ribonucleotides).
  • Dietary Need: Nonessential. Humans synthesize de novo.
  • Inhibitors: Act as anti-tumor/viral/bacterial drugs by stopping cell division.

2. De Novo Precursors

  • Glycine: C4, C5, N7
  • Aspartate: N1
  • Glutamine: N3, N9
  • THF (Folate): C2, C8
  • CO₂: C6

3. Key De Novo Enzymes

  • Start: Ribose-5-P → PRPP (PRPP Synthetase)
  • Rate-Limiting/Committed: PRPP → 5-phosphoribosylamine (PRPP-amidotransferase)
  • Parent Nucleotide: IMP (Inosine Monophosphate)

4. Regulation

  • Activator: High PRPP concentration.
  • Feedback Inhibition: AMP & GMP inhibit both PRPP synthetase and PRPP-amidotransferase.
  • AMP inhibits its own synthesis from IMP; GMP does the same.

5. Salvage Pathway

Recycles free purines + PRPP → Nucleotides

  • APRTase: Rescues Adenine → AMP.
  • HGPRTase: Rescues Hypoxanthine → IMP and Guanine → GMP.

6. Catabolism (Degradation)

  • 👉 End Product: Uric Acid (sparingly soluble)
  • 👉 Key Enzyme 1: Adenosine Deaminase (Adenosine → Inosine)
  • 👉 Key Enzyme 2: Xanthine Oxidase (Converts Hypoxanthine & Xanthine into Uric Acid, reducing O₂ to H₂O₂)
