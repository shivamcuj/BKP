Part 1: Detailed Explanations

1. Introduction to the Central Dogma

Formulated by Francis Crick, the Central Dogma of Molecular Biology defines the three major steps in the processing of genetic information:

  1. Replication: Copying parent DNA to form identical daughter DNA molecules.
  2. Transcription: Rewriting the genetic message from DNA into RNA.
  3. Translation: Translating the RNA message into a protein structure via ribosomes.
DNA RNA Protein

2. What is DNA Replication?

To transfer genetic information during cellular reproduction, DNA must be duplicated. This process follows a semiconservative mechanism.

💡 Analogy: Imagine copying a handwritten recipe. You keep one original page and pair it with a newly written page. In DNA, each replicated daughter molecule contains one parent strand and one newly synthesized strand.

3. The Builders: DNA Polymerases

DNA synthesis is catalyzed by DNA-dependent DNA polymerases. They require a DNA template to work.

Prokaryotic Polymerases:

  • Pol I: Completes chain synthesis between Okazaki fragments on the lagging strand (fills gaps) and removes RNA primers.
  • Pol II: Mostly concerned with proofreading and DNA repair.
  • Pol III: The main workhorse! Catalyzes the synthesis of both the leading and lagging strands.

Eukaryotic Polymerases (The Greek Letters):

  • α (Alpha): Gap filling and synthesis between Okazaki fragments.
  • ε (Epsilon): DNA proofreading and DNA repair.
  • β (Beta): DNA repair.
  • γ (Gamma): Mitochondrial DNA synthesis.
  • δ (Delta): Leading and lagging strand synthesis.

4. The Three Stages of Replication

A Initiation (The Setup)

Occurs at a specific site called the Origin of Replication (Ori). In prokaryotes, there is one Ori; in eukaryotes, there are multiple consensus sequences.

  • DNA A protein: Recognizes and binds to "ori" to start denaturing DNA.
  • Helicase (DNA B protein): Unwinds parental DNA, forming a 'V' shape known as the Replication Fork.
  • Topoisomerase: Relieves the twisting stress caused by unwinding by cutting and rejoining DNA strands.
  • SSB (Single-Stranded Binding) proteins: Stabilize separated strands so they don't snap back together.
  • Primase (RNA polymerase): Creates short RNA primers (5-50 nucleotides) to kickstart DNA synthesis, as DNA Pol III cannot start from scratch.
💡 Analogy: Helicase is like a zipper unzipping your jacket. Topoisomerase is you untangling the knotted strings on your hoodie.

B Elongation (The Building Phase)

DNA Pol III adds nucleotides to the 3' end of the RNA primer. DNA is always synthesized in the 5' to 3' direction.

  • Leading Strand: The 3'→5' template is copied continuously towards the replication fork. Requires only one primer.
  • Lagging Strand: The 5'→3' template is copied discontinuously (away from the fork) in short segments called Okazaki fragments. Requires multiple RNA primers.
  • Cleanup: DNA Pol I removes the RNA primers and fills the gaps. Finally, DNA ligase acts as molecular glue, sealing the Okazaki fragments together with phosphodiester bonds.

C Termination (The End)

Specific termination sequences (e.g., "ter") exist. A ter-binding protein binds here, stopping the helicase from unwinding further, thus safely concluding the process.

5. Proofreading (Quality Control)

DNA replication has high fidelity. The error rate is kept incredibly low (1 in 108 to 1012 bases) via proofreading.

  • All three prokaryotic DNA polymerases have a 3' to 5' exonuclease activity (backspacing to fix typos).
  • DNA Pol I and II specialize in excising mismatched nucleotides before adding the next one.

Part 2: Quick Revision Notes

Perfect for that last-minute scan right before your exam! Take a deep breath, you've got this.

🧬 Core Concepts

  • Central Dogma: DNA → RNA → Protein.
  • Mechanism: Semiconservative (1 old strand + 1 new strand).
  • Synthesis Direction: Always 5' → 3'.
  • Leading Strand: Continuous synthesis.
  • Lagging Strand: Discontinuous, forms Okazaki fragments.

🛠️ Enzyme Cheat Sheet

  • Helicase: Unwinds DNA.
  • Topoisomerase: Relieves unwinding stress.
  • SSB Proteins: Keeps strands separated.
  • Primase: Lays down RNA primer.
  • Pol III / δ (Delta): Main builder.
  • Pol I / α (Alpha): Removes primers, fills gaps.
  • Ligase: Seals gaps (the glue).

🌍 Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic Replication

Feature Prokaryotes Eukaryotes
RNA Primer Length ~50 nucleotides ~9 nucleotides
DNA Polymerases 3 Types (I, II, III) 5 Types (α, β, γ, δ, ε)
Origins of Replication Single Multiple
Okazaki Fragment Length 1000 - 2000 nucleotides ~200 nucleotides
Rate of Replication ~500 nucleotides/sec ~50 nucleotides/sec (10x slower)
