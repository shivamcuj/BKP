DNA Repair Mechanisms

Damage to DNA caused by replication errors or mutations may have serious consequences. The cell possesses an inbuilt system to repair damaged DNA to maintain genetic integrity.

I. Detailed Explanations

1. Base Excision-Repair

Certain bases can undergo spontaneous depurination or alteration (e.g., cytosine, adenine, and guanine forming uracil, hypoxanthine, and xanthine). Because these altered bases do not belong in normal DNA, they must be excised and replaced.

✦ Mechanism Steps:

  • ● Recognition and removal of the defective base by N-glycosylase.
  • ● An endonuclease cuts the DNA backbone near the defect, removing the abasic sugar.
  • ● The gap is filled by DNA polymerase.
  • ● The nick is sealed by DNA ligase.

Specific Example

If cytosine is deaminated to form uracil, the enzyme uracil DNA glycosylase identifies and removes the uracil base. The remaining backbone is cut, cleared, and the correct cytosine is re-inserted and sealed.

2. Nucleotide Excision-Repair

This mechanism repairs large-scale defects such as modifications, strand breaks, or cross-linkages caused by environmental factors like ultraviolet (UV) light and ionizing radiation.

✦ Mechanism Steps:

  • ● Identification of the defective DNA segment and unwinding of the double helix.
  • ● An exinuclease (excision nuclease) cuts the DNA on both sides (upstream and downstream) of the damage.
  • ● The defective fragment (approx. 30 nt length) is degraded.
  • ● DNA polymerase fills the gap, and DNA ligase seals it.

Clinical Example

Xeroderma pigmentosum (XP): A rare autosomal recessive disease characterized by extreme photosensitivity and a high susceptibility to skin cancers. It is caused by a genetic defect in the nucleotide excision repair system.

3. Mismatch Repair

Corrects copying errors made during replication (e.g., incorporating C instead of T opposite to A, creating 1-5 base unpaired loops).

✦ Mechanism Steps:

  • ● The repair system distinguishes the template strand (which is methylated) from the newly synthesized strand (unmethylated).
  • ● GATC endonuclease cuts the new strand at an adjacent methylated GATC sequence.
  • ● An exonuclease digests the defective strand to remove the mismatch.
  • ● DNA polymerase and ligase synthesize and seal the new strand.

Clinical Example

Hereditary nonpolyposis colon cancer (HNPCC): One of the most common inherited cancers, which is directly linked to a faulty mismatch repair system in the DNA.

4. Double-Strand Break (DSB) Repair

DSBs are highly dangerous and can lead to genetic recombination, chromosomal translocation, broken chromosomes, and cell death. They are typically caused by ionizing radiations, free radicals, or chemotherapy.

✦ Repair Mechanisms:

Repair involves unwinding, alignment, and ligation, accomplished via two main pathways:

1.

Homologous Recombination: Dominant mechanism occurring primarily in yeasts.

2.

Non-Homologous End Joining: The dominant repair mechanism in mammals.

II. Quick Revision Notes

Perfect for your last-minute exam prep! ✨

1. Base Excision

  • Damage: Single base alteration (spontaneous, chemical, radiation).
  • Key Enzymes: N-glycosylase, Endonuclease, DNA Pol, Ligase.
  • Process: Remove base → remove sugar → replace → seal.
  • Example: C deaminates to U → Uracil DNA glycosylase fixes it.

2. Nucleotide Excision

  • Damage: DNA segment damage (UV, radiation, cross-links).
  • Key Enzymes: Exinuclease, DNA Pol, Ligase.
  • Process: Cut 30 nt segment (upstream & downstream) → replace.
  • Clinical: Xeroderma pigmentosum (XP) - photosensitive, skin cancer risk.

3. Mismatch Repair

  • Damage: Copying errors (unpaired loops).
  • Identification: Template strand is methylated, new strand isn't.
  • Key Enzymes: GATC endonuclease, Exonuclease.
  • Clinical: Hereditary nonpolyposis colon cancer (HNPCC).

4. Double-Strand Break

  • Damage: Broken chromosomes from ionizing radiation/chemo.
  • Risk: Chromosomal translocation, cell death.
  • Mammals: Non-homologous end joining dominates.
  • Yeasts: Homologous recombination dominates.
