Part 1: Detailed Explanations

Introduction to Translation

The pathway of protein biosynthesis is called translation. It is the poetic process by which ribosomes convert the information carried by mRNA—written in the form of a genetic code—into the synthesis of a brand new protein.

  • Translation occurs in the cytosol on ribosomes and is intricately guided by mRNA.
  • While the basic blueprint is similar across all life forms, eukaryotic translation involves more protein components and delicate, intricate steps compared to prokaryotes.

Eukaryotes vs. Prokaryotes

Characters Eukaryotes Prokaryotes
Ribosome Larger, 80S (consists of 60S and 40S subunits) Smaller, 70S (consists of 50S and 30S subunits)

Start Signal Initiating AUG of mRNA is preceded by a methylguanosyl triphosphate cap Initiating AUG of mRNA is preceded by a purine-rich sequence (Shine-Dalgarno)

Termination Factors Carried out by a single release factor (eRF) Carried out by three releasing factors (RF1, RF2, RF3)

Basic Requirements for Translation

Before the magical assembly of a protein begins, the cell gathers these essential ingredients:

  • mRNA: The messenger carrying the genetic recipe.
  • tRNAs: The dedicated carriers bringing specific amino acids.
  • Ribosomes: The workbench where assembly happens.
  • Energy: Sourced from ATP and GTP.
  • Enzymes & Factors: E.g., aminoacyl tRNA synthases, initiation factors, and elongation factors.

The 4 Beautiful Stages of Eukaryotic Translation

1 Activation of Amino Acids

Taking place in the cytosol, this is where amino acids are "woken up" and prepared for their journey. Each of the 20 amino acids is covalently attached to their specific tRNA.

Concept Clarification: This attachment costs energy (ATP) and is facilitated by the enzyme aminoacyl tRNA synthase (AAS). Once attached, it forms aminoacyl tRNA, which uses its anticodon to recognize the exact matching codon on the mRNA.

2 Initiation

Initiation is a highly coordinated dance involving four precise steps:

  • Ribosomal Dissociation: The 80S ribosome splits into 40S and 60S subunits, aided by factors eIF3 and eIF-1A. This opens the A (aminoacyl) and P (peptidyl) binding sites.
  • Formation of 43S Pre-initiation Complex: GTP and eIF2 bind to the initiator tRNA (Met-tRNAMet). This complex then attaches to the 40S subunit.
  • Formation of 48S Initiation Complex: The mRNA, capped at its 5' end, binds to the 43S complex. This step requires Cap Binding Protein (eIF4) and ATP.
  • Formation of 80S Initiation Complex: The large 60S subunit rejoins the group. GTP is hydrolyzed, releasing initiation factors. The initiator tRNA is beautifully positioned at the P site, paired with the start codon (AUG).

3 Elongation

The polypeptide chain grows through a repeating three-step cycle:

  • Binding to A Site: The next aminoacyl tRNA, guided by elongation factor eEF-1α and GTP, enters the A site.
  • Peptide Bond Formation: The enzyme peptidyl transferase transfers the amino acid from the P site to the new amino acid in the A site, forging a peptide bond.
  • Translocation: The ribosome shifts exactly one codon (three bases) towards the 3' end of the mRNA. The empty tRNA exits, the growing chain moves to the P site, and the A site opens up for the next arrival. This requires factor eEF2 and GTP.

4 Termination

The cycle repeats until a nonsense/termination codon (UAA, UAG, or UGA) glides into the A site.

Because no tRNA matches these stop signals, a single eukaryotic release factor (eRF) binds instead. With the help of GTP, it triggers the hydrolysis of the bond holding the polypeptide to the tRNA. The newly minted protein floats free, the mRNA is released, and the ribosome gently dissociates back into 60S and 40S subunits, ready for a new beginning.

Part 2: Quick Revision Notes ✨

🎯 Core Concepts

  • Definition: Conversion of mRNA genetic code into protein by ribosomes.
  • Location: Cytosol, on ribosomes.
  • Key Ingredients: mRNA, tRNA, Ribosomes (80S in eukaryotes), Energy (ATP/GTP), Enzymes.
  • Eukaryote start signal: Capped 5' end.
  • Prokaryote start signal: Shine-Dalgarno sequence.

🚀 1. Activation & 2. Initiation

  • Activation: Amino acid + tRNA + ATP → Aminoacyl tRNA (Enzyme: Aminoacyl tRNA synthase).
  • Initiation:
    • 80S splits to 40S & 60S.
    • 43S Complex: 40S + eIF2 + GTP + Met-tRNA.
    • 48S Complex: 43S + mRNA (requires Cap Binding Protein).
    • 80S Complex: 48S + 60S. Met-tRNA is safely in the P site.

🧬 3. Elongation

  • Bind to A site: Next aminoacyl tRNA enters A site (uses eEF-1α & GTP).
  • Peptide Bond: Formed by peptidyl transferase. Chain transfers from P site to A site.
  • Translocation: Ribosome moves 1 codon (3 bases) towards 3' end. Empty tRNA leaves. Growing chain moves to P site. (Uses eEF2 & GTP).

🛑 4. Termination

  • Stop Codons: UAA, UAG, UGA enter the A site.
  • Release Factor: Single factor eRF binds (Prokaryotes use RF1, 2, 3).
  • Action: Hydrolysis of peptidyl tRNA bond.
  • Result: Polypeptide is freed, mRNA leaves, ribosome splits to 40S and 60S for recycling.
