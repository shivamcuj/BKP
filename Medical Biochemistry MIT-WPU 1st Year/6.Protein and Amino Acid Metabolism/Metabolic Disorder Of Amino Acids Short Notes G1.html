Introduction

There are 20 amino acids involved in metabolism, 9 of which are essential (cannot be synthesized in humans and must be obtained through food).

Amino acids act as the building blocks of proteins. They are also vital precursors for important molecules in the body, such as:

Neurotransmitters
Hormones
Pigments
O₂ Carriers

Hereditary Metabolic Disorders: These occur when defective genes (usually autosomal recessive) are passed from parents to children. They result from defects in either the breakdown of amino acids or the transport of amino acids into cells. They are typically diagnosed by analyzing amino acid concentrations in body fluids.

Section I: Detailed Notes

Topic 01

Phenylketonuria (PKU)

The Defect

Caused by decreased activity of Phenylalanine Hydroxylase (PAH), an enzyme that converts the amino acid phenylalanine to tyrosine.

Note on Tyrosine: It is a crucial precursor for hormones, skin, hair, and eye pigments (melanin).

Malignant PKU: A rare variant caused by dihydrobiopterin reductase deficiency (needed to convert dihydro to tetrahydrobiopterin, a cofactor for PAH). This also leads to a deficiency of dopamine and serotonin.

Symptoms & Characteristics

  • Accumulation of phenylalanine and its neurotoxic metabolites (phenylketones).
  • Progressive developmental delay, small head circumference (microcephaly), seizures, and behavioral disturbances.
  • Mousy odor, eczema, vomiting.
  • Physical features: Lighter features (blond hair, blue eyes) compared to family members, due to decreased melanin.

Treatment Plan:

Dietary restriction of phenylalanine, tyrosine supplementation, special formulas. Medications like Sapropterin (cofactor supplementation) and Pegvaliase (to lyse phenylalanine).

Topic 02

Tyrosinemia

An inborn error where the body cannot effectively break down tyrosine, leading to toxic accumulations.

Type I (Classic/Hepatorenal)

Deficiency: Fumarylacetoacetate hydrolase (FAH)

Most severe. Severe liver disease, kidney defects, cabbage-like odor, rickets. ~40% develop liver cancer by age 5 if untreated.

Type II

Deficiency: Tyrosine aminotransferase (TAT)

Presents in early childhood. Eye pain, redness, photophobia. Palmoplantar hyperkeratosis (thick painful skin on palms and soles), intellectual disabilities.

Type III

Deficiency: 4-hydroxyphenylpyruvate dioxygenase (HPD)

Intellectual disability, seizures, and intermittent ataxia.

Treatment Plan:

Protein restriction, low tyrosine diet. Nitisinone (NTBC) is a potent inhibitor of the pathway that prevents toxic metabolites. Liver transplantation may be required for severe cases or cancer.

Topic 03

Homocystinuria

The Defect

Defect in Cystathionine beta-synthase (CBS) (requires Pyridoxine/Vit B6), affecting methionine metabolism and leading to homocysteine accumulation.

Symptoms & Characteristics

  • Endothelial damage leading to thrombosis (vascular disease).
  • Tall, thin frame with Marfanoid features.
  • Dislocated lens (myopia) and osteoporosis (thinning bones).
  • Pronounced flush of the cheeks.
  • Intellectual disability and psychiatric disorders.

Treatment Plan:

High doses of Vitamin B6 (pyridoxine) helps ~50% of patients. Folic acid, Betaine (removes extra homocysteine), aspirin (for thrombosis risk), methionine restriction, and cysteine supplementation.

Topic 04

Non-ketotic Hyperglycinemia (NKH)

The Defect

A rare genetic disorder caused by a defect in the Glycine Cleavage System. This results in the massive accumulation of the amino acid/neurotransmitter glycine in tissues, fluids, and especially the central nervous system.

Symptoms & Treatment

  • Severe developmental impairment.
  • Seizures, low muscle tone (hypotonia).
  • Hiccups and breath-holding spells.
Treatment: No cure. Symptoms eased by glycine-blocking drugs (Dextromethorphan), low-protein diet, and glycine scavengers (Sodium benzoate).
Topic 05

Maple Syrup Urine Disease (MSUD)

The Defect

Deficiency of Branched-chain alpha-ketoacid dehydrogenase.

This affects the breakdown of the branched-chain amino acids: Leucine, Isoleucine, and Valine. These, along with their oxoacids (e.g., alloisoleucine), accumulate in body fluids.

Symptoms & Characteristics

  • Characteristic "maple syrup" smell to the urine.
  • Presents in infancy with poor feeding/irritability, progressing to lethargy, cerebral edema, seizures, coma, and potentially death.
  • Common in the Mennonites of Pennsylvania.

Treatment Plan:

Strict dietary restriction of branched-chain amino acids (leucine, isoleucine, valine). Hemodialysis for acute crises. Milder forms may respond to high-dose Thiamin (Vitamin B1).

Alkaptonuria Extra

Enzyme: Homogentisate Oxidase

Homogentisic acid accumulates. Urine turns dark/black when exposed to air.

  • Pigmentation of connective tissue (sclera, ear cartilage).
  • Arthritis (joints/spine), cardiac valve involvement, kidney stones.

Rx: Nitisinone, restriction of tyrosine/phenylalanine, Vitamin C.

Albinism Extra

Enzyme: Tyrosinase

Absence of melanin pigment in melanocytes.

  • Colorless hair and skin.
  • Lack of eye pigment causing photophobia.
  • Increased risk of skin cancer.
  • No neurological deficits (unlike PKU).
