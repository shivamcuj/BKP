Protein & Amino Acid Metabolism

"Understanding the dynamic balance of life's building blocks."

Detailed Explanations

Medical & Biological Importance

Proteins form the structural and functional foundation of our bodies. A typical 70 kg adult contains about 12 kg of protein.

  • Continuous Turnover: About 400 gm of body protein is synthesized and degraded daily (approx. 6 gm/kg/day). This continuous re-synthesis acts as a quality control mechanism to remove aged, damaged, or non-functional proteins.
  • Adaptation: Protein degradation increases during starvation, diabetes, and tissue injury. It is vital for shaping tissues during pregnancy and cellular adaptation.
  • Amino Acid Pool: Normal plasma concentration is 40–60 mg%. Excess amino acids cannot be stored; they are degraded. The amino group is extracted as toxic ammonia (later converted to urea in the liver), and the carbon skeleton is oxidized for energy, glucose formation (gluconeogenesis), or fat synthesis.

Protein Turnover

Body proteins are in a dynamic state. Turnover is influenced by physiological needs (high synthesis during growth/lactation, high degradation during fever/starvation).

Mechanisms of Control:

  • Ubiquitin: A small polypeptide (mol. wt. 8,500) that tags proteins for destruction.
  • PEST Sequence: Proteins rich in Proline, E (Glutamate/Glutamine), Serine, and Threonine are rapidly degraded.

Nitrogen Balance

Evaluates the relationship between dietary nitrogen intake (protein) and nitrogen excretion.

  • 1. Equilibrium: Intake = Excretion. Seen in normal, healthy adults.
  • 2. Positive (+ve): Intake > Excretion. Protein is laid down. Seen in growing infants and pregnant women.
  • 3. Negative (-ve): Intake < Excretion. Seen in severe illness, major trauma, advanced cancer, or starvation (Kwashiorkor/Marasmus).

Stages of Amino Acid Catabolism

The complete breakdown of an amino acid follows three distinct stages:

Stage 1

Removal of $\alpha$-amino group

Occurs via Transamination (shifting the group) and Deamination (liberating the group as $NH_3$).

Stage 2

Disposal of Ammonia

Toxic ammonia ($NH_3$) is transported to the liver and converted into relatively non-toxic Urea.

Stage 3

Carbon Skeleton Catabolism

The remaining keto acid is oxidized in the Citric Acid Cycle for $CO_2$, $H_2O$, and energy.

Transamination

The transfer of an amino ($-NH_2$) group from an amino acid to a keto acid. It involves the interconversion of a pair of amino acids and a pair of keto acids, catalyzed by transaminases (aminotransferases).

Salient Features

  • Requires Pyridoxal Phosphate (PLP) (Vitamin B6 derivative) as a coenzyme.
  • Does not liberate free $NH_3$; it only transfers it.
  • Highly reversible process.
  • Concentrates nitrogen in Glutamate, which then undergoes oxidative deamination.
  • All amino acids participate EXCEPT Lysine, Threonine, Proline, and Hydroxyproline.

Mechanism (Ping Pong Bi Bi)

  1. The enzyme's PLP forms a Schiff base (imine linkage) with a lysine residue.
  2. Incoming amino acid binds, displacing lysine to form an amino-acid-PLP-Schiff base.
  3. Amino group is transferred to PLP, forming pyridoxamine phosphate and releasing a new keto acid.
  4. A new keto acid binds, receives the amino group, forming a new amino acid, and regenerating the enzyme-PLP complex.

Deamination

The direct removal of the amino group as free $NH_3$ (ammonia). Occurs simultaneously with transamination (often called transdeamination).

1. Oxidative Deamination

Liberation of $NH_3$ coupled with oxidation, mostly in the liver and kidney.

Glutamate Dehydrogenase (GDH)

Glutamate serves as the "collection center". GDH converts Glutamate $\rightleftharpoons$ $\alpha$-Ketoglutarate + $NH_3$. Unique feature: Uses either $NAD^+$ or $NADP^+$ as a coenzyme.

Regulation: Inhibited by ATP, GTP, Steroids. Activated by ADP, GDP.

Amino Acid Oxidases

Flavoproteins (L-amino acid oxidase uses FMN; D-amino acid oxidase uses FAD). Generates $H_2O_2$ (decomposed by catalase). D-amino acid oxidase is vital for converting unnatural D-amino acids from diet into usable L-forms.

2. Non-Oxidative Deamination

Liberation of $NH_3$ without oxidation.

  • Dehydratases (PLP dependent): Acts on hydroxy amino acids (Serine $\rightarrow$ Pyruvate + $NH_3$, Threonine, Homoserine).
  • Desulfhydrases: Acts on sulfur amino acids (Cysteine $\rightarrow$ Pyruvate + $H_2S$ + $NH_3$).
  • Histidase: Deaminates Histidine $\rightarrow$ Urocanate + $NH_3$.

Decarboxylation

The removal of $CO_2$ from the carboxyl group of an amino acid to form the corresponding amine. This process requires Pyridoxal Phosphate (PLP).

Amino Acid
Decarboxylase (PLP) Amine + $CO_2$

Physiologically Significant Biogenic Amines (Examples):

  • Histidine $\rightarrow$ Histamine
  • Tryptophan $\rightarrow$ Tryptamine
  • Tyrosine $\rightarrow$ Tyramine

Quick Revision Notes

1. Fundamentals & Turnover

  • Body Protein: 12kg in 70kg adult.
  • Turnover: ~400g synthesized/degraded daily.
  • Tagging for destruction: Done by Ubiquitin.
  • PEST Sequence: Proline, Glutamate, Serine, Threonine = rapid degradation.

2. Nitrogen Balance

  • Equilibrium: Intake = Excretion (Normal adult).
  • Positive (+): Intake > Excretion (Growth, pregnancy).
  • Negative (-): Intake < Excretion (Starvation, illness, Kwashiorkor).

3. Transamination

  • Action: Swaps $-NH_2$ group between amino acid and keto acid.
  • Coenzyme: PLP (Vitamin B6). forms Schiff base.
  • Mechanism: Ping Pong Bi Bi.
  • Exceptions: Lysine, Threonine, Proline, Hydroxyproline do NOT participate.
  • Hub: Concentrates nitrogen into Glutamate.

4. Deamination

  • Action: Removes $-NH_2$ as free $NH_3$.
  • Oxidative:
    • GDH: Glutamate $\rightarrow$ $\alpha$-Ketoglutarate. Regulated by ATP/GTP (-) and ADP/GDP (+). Uses $NAD^+$/$NADP^+$.
    • Oxidases: D-amino acid oxidase (FAD) converts unnatural diet D-forms to L-forms.
  • Non-Oxidative: Dehydratases (Serine/Threonine), Desulfhydrases (Cysteine), Histidase.

5. Decarboxylation & Overall Catabolism

Decarboxylation:
  • Removes $CO_2$, forming biogenic amines.
  • Requires PLP.
  • Ex: Histidine $\rightarrow$ Histamine.
3 Catabolic Stages:
  1. Remove Amino Group (Transamination/Deamination).
  2. Dispose Ammonia (Urea Cycle in Liver).
  3. Dispose Carbon Skeleton (TCA cycle for energy/glucose/fat).
