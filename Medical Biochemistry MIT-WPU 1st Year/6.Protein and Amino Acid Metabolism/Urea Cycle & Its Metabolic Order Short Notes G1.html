I Detailed Explanations

Deep dive into the Krebs-Henseleit cycle

1. Introduction to the Urea Cycle

Ammonia is highly toxic to the Central Nervous System (CNS), even in microscopic traces. To protect the body, the liver acts as a vital filtration hub, rapidly removing ammonia from circulation and converting it into a non-toxic, water-soluble compound known as Urea.

  • Alternative Name: The Krebs-Henseleit cycle (named after the scientists who proposed it).
  • Site of Synthesis: Predominantly the liver. Reactions occur across both the mitochondria and the cytosol.
  • Nature of Process: It is highly energy-dependent (requires ATP).
  • Amino Acid Intermediates: Interestingly, the intermediates in this cycle are amino acids for which no genetic codons exist (e.g., Ornithine, Citrulline).

✨ Concept Clarification: Think of ammonia as a hazardous exhaust fume generated by protein breakdown. The liver acts like a catalytic converter, using energy (ATP) to safely package these fumes into a harmless fluid (urea) that the kidneys can easily flush away.

2. The 5 Reactions of Urea Formation

Note: The first reaction is a preparatory step essential for the cycle, while the remaining four constitute the actual cyclic process.

Step 1: Formation of Carbamoyl Phosphate

📍 Location: Mitochondria
🧬 Enzyme: Carbamoyl Phosphate Synthetase-I

Ammonia condenses with HCO3- at the expense of 2 high-energy bonds (2 ATP → 2 ADP + 2 Pi) to form carbamoyl phosphate. One Pi is consumed.

Requirement: Needs N-acetyl glutamate (activator) and Mg2+. This is a thermodynamically strong step that pulls the entire cycle forward.

Step 2: Formation of Citrulline

📍 Location: Mitochondria → Cytosol
🧬 Enzyme: Ornithine Transcarbamoylase

Carbamoyl phosphate condenses with ornithine to form citrulline. Citrulline then crosses the inner mitochondrial membrane into the cytosol via a specific transporter.

Step 3: Formation of Argininosuccinate

📍 Location: Cytosol
🧬 Enzyme: Argininosuccinate Synthetase

Citrulline condenses with aspartate (which provides the second nitrogen for urea). This consumes 2 high-energy bonds (ATP → AMP + PPi, and PPi is further hydrolyzed to 2 Pi).

Step 4: Cleavage to Arginine and Fumarate

📍 Location: Cytosol
🧬 Enzyme: Argininosuccinase (Argininosuccinate lyase)

Argininosuccinate is cleaved, resulting in arginine (continues in cycle) and fumarate (exits cycle).

Fate of Fumarate: Recycled back to aspartate (Fumarate → Malate → Oxaloacetate → Aspartate) linking to the TCA cycle.

Step 5: Formation of Urea and Ornithine

📍 Location: Cytosol
🧬 Enzyme: Arginase

Arginine is hydrolyzed to produce Urea and regenerate Ornithine. Ornithine re-enters the mitochondria to start the cycle anew.

Overall Equation for Urea Formation

NH3 + HCO3- + Aspartate + 4ATP → Urea + Fumarate + 4ADP + 4Pi

3. Metabolic Disorders of the Urea Cycle

These are inherited genetic defects with an incidence of about 1 in 2500. Defective enzymes lead to ammonia intoxication (hyperammonemia). Symptoms include vomiting, lethargy, irritability, mental retardation, and coma.

Disorder Defective Enzyme Accumulated Product
Hyperammonemia Type-I Carbamoyl phosphate synthetase-I Ammonia
Hyperammonemia Type-II
(Most common, causes orotic acid in urine)		 Ornithine transcarbamoylase Ammonia (diverted to pyrimidines)
Citrullinemia Argininosuccinate synthetase Citrulline
Argininosuccinic aciduria Argininosuccinate lyase (Argininosuccinase) Argininosuccinate
Argininemia Arginase Arginine
N-Acetylglutamate Synthetase Deficiency N-acetyl glutamate synthetase Ammonia (Hyperammonemia)

💊 Clinical Treatment:

Treatments focus on removing excess ammonia and reducing dietary nitrogen. Peritoneal dialysis clears blood ammonia. Administration of Benzoic acid and Phenyl acetate increases alternative nitrogen excretion pathways.

II Quick Revision Notes

High-yield bullet points for last-minute exam prep

📌 Core Facts

  • Site: Liver (Mitochondria & Cytosol).
  • Purpose: Convert toxic $NH_3$ to non-toxic Urea.
  • Regulation: Rate-limiting enzyme is Carbamoyl Phosphate Synthetase-I, activated by N-acetylglutamate.
  • Nitrogen Sources: 1 from free Ammonia, 1 from Aspartate.
  • Carbon Source: $HCO_3^-$ (from $CO_2$).
  • Blood Urea Norm: 16 - 36 mg/100 ml.
  • Excretion: 10-25 gm/day in urine.

🔄 The 5 Steps (Mnemonics)

Mnemonic for Intermediates: "Ordinarily Careless Crappers Are Also Frivolous About Urination"

Ornithine → Carbamoyl phosphate → Citrulline → Aspartate → Argininosuccinate → Fumarate → Arginine → Urea

  1. Ammonia + $CO_2$ → Carbamoyl PO4 (Uses 2 ATP, Mito)
  2. + Ornithine → Citrulline (Mito → Cyto)
  3. + Aspartate → Argininosuccinate (Uses ATP, Cyto)
  4. Cleavage → Arginine + Fumarate (Cyto)
  5. Arginine hydrolysis → Urea + Ornithine (Cyto)
