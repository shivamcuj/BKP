Oxidative Phosphorylation

A comprehensive guide to understanding biological energy generation, tailored for clarity, retention, and a touch of elegance.

Comprehensive Explanations

1. What is Oxidative Phosphorylation?

The formation of ATP from ADP and inorganic phosphate (Pi) is termed Phosphorylation. Because this phosphorylation is directly coupled with biological oxidation within the body, the overall process is called biological oxidative phosphorylation.

2. Chemiosmotic Theory (Mechanism)

Proposed by British biochemist Peter Mitchell, this is the most widely accepted hypothesis explaining the mechanism of oxidative phosphorylation. It states that the energy released from oxidation generates an electrochemical potential by pumping protons across the inner mitochondrial membrane, which is then converted into ATP.

Three Basic Principles of the Theory:

  • ✿ Proton Pumping: The major electron carriers are organized into three complexes (Complex I, III, and IV) spanning the inner mitochondrial membrane. As electrons are transported, the energy released pumps protons (H+) from the mitochondrial matrix to the intermembrane space.
  • ✿ Electrochemical Potential: Because the inner mitochondrial membrane is impermeable to protons, the pumping action generates a significant electrochemical gradient (a difference in pH and charge).
  • ✿ ATP Synthesis: Driven by the proton-motive force, H+ ions flow back into the matrix through the F0F1 ATPase molecule. The free energy released by this downward flow is coupled with the phosphorylation of ADP to form ATP.

3. The P:O Ratio

The P:O ratio measures the number of high-energy phosphates (ATP molecules) synthesized per atom of oxygen consumed or per molecule of water produced.

  • Oxidation of metabolites yielding NADH gives a P:O ratio of 3 (Generates 3 ATP).
  • Oxidation of metabolites yielding FADH2 gives a P:O ratio of 2 (Generates 2 ATP).

4. Substrate Level Phosphorylation

This is the formation of ATP directly coupled to a metabolic process, completely bypassing the electron transport chain (ETC) and molecular oxygen (O2).

Examples of Substrate Level Phosphorylation:

5. Shuttle Systems for Extramitochondrial NADH

While most NADH and FADH2 are generated inside the mitochondria (via Krebs cycle and β-oxidation), NADH generated by glycolysis in the cytosol cannot cross the impermeable inner mitochondrial membrane. Special shuttle systems carry reducing equivalents (not the NADH itself) into the mitochondria.

A) The Malate-Aspartate Shuttle

  • Cytosolic NADH transfers electrons to oxaloacetate, forming malate (via cytosolic malate dehydrogenase).
  • Malate crosses the inner membrane via a dicarboxylate transporter.
  • Inside the matrix, malate transfers electrons to mitochondrial NAD+, reforming NADH and oxaloacetate.
  • This matrix NADH enters the ETC, generating 3 ATP.
  • Oxaloacetate is converted to aspartate to shuttle back out to the cytosol, completing the cycle.

B) The Glycerol Phosphate Shuttle

  • Cytosolic NADH transfers electrons to dihydroxyacetone phosphate (DHAP), forming glycerol-3-phosphate.
  • Glycerol-3-phosphate diffuses into the intermembrane space.
  • It transfers its electrons to an FAD-containing enzyme on the inner membrane, forming FADH2 and reforming DHAP.
  • The FADH2 enters the ETC, generating 2 ATP.
  • DHAP returns to the cytosol to be reused.

Last-Minute Revision Notes

Definitions

  • Oxidative Phosphorylation: ATP formation from ADP + Pi, coupled with biological oxidation.
  • Substrate Level Phosphorylation: Direct ATP formation without ETC/O2. (Ex: PEP → Pyruvate, Succinyl-CoA → Succinate).
  • P:O Ratio: ATP made per Oxygen atom consumed.

Energy Yields

1 NADH = 3 ATP
1 FADH2 = 2 ATP

Chemiosmotic Theory (Mitchell)

  • 1. Pump: Complexes I, III, IV pump H+ from matrix to intermembrane space.
  • 2. Gradient: Inner membrane is impermeable; creates electrochemical potential.
  • 3. ATP Synthesis: H+ flows back via F0F1 ATPase, releasing energy to form ATP.

Cytosolic NADH Shuttles

  • Malate-Aspartate Shuttle: Yields NADH in matrix → 3 ATP.
  • Glycerol Phosphate Shuttle: Yields FADH2 in matrix → 2 ATP.
