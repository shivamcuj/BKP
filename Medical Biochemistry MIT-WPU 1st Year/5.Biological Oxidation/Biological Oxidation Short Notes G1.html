Biological Oxidation

"Understanding the energy of life, beautifully."

Part I: Detailed Explanations

Introduction

Oxidation occurring within living systems is elegantly termed biological oxidation. These reactions are inherently exergonic, meaning they release energy.

  • During these oxidations, reacting chemical systems move from a higher energy level to a lower one, liberating energy.
  • The energy, instead of merely dissipating as heat, is beautifully captured and converted into chemical energy through the formation of the energy-rich compound ATP.
  • Biological Oxidative Phosphorylation: The formation of ATP from ADP and Pi ($Phosphorylation$) is coupled with biological oxidation.

Coenzyme Systems & Enzymes

Biological oxidation is orchestrated by an elegant interplay of specific enzymes and their coenzymes. The enzymes involved belong to the class of Oxidoreductases.

The 4 Classes of Enzymes

  • Oxidases
  • Dehydrogenases
  • Hydroperoxidases
  • Oxygenases

Essential Coenzymes

  • NAD$^+$ (Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide)
  • NADP$^+$ (Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate)
  • FMN (Flavin mononucleotide)
  • FAD (Flavin adenine dinucleotide)

1 Oxidases

Oxidases catalyze the removal of hydrogen from a substrate, gracefully transferring it to oxygen. Here, oxygen ($O_2$) acts as the ultimate hydrogen acceptor, forming either water ($H_2O$) or hydrogen peroxide ($H_2O_2$).

Beautiful Examples:

  • Cytochrome oxidase (Crucial in the final step of the electron transport chain)
  • L-amino acid oxidases
  • Xanthine oxidase

2 Dehydrogenases

These enzymes also remove hydrogen from a substrate, but unlike oxidases, they cannot use oxygen as a hydrogen acceptor. Instead, they rely on specific coenzymes to accept the hydrogen atoms.

Nicotinamide Coenzymes (NAD$^+$ or NADP$^+$) Linked

Derived from Vitamin Niacin

They remove two hydrogen atoms. One is transferred to the coenzyme, and the other appears as a hydrogen ion ($H^+$) in the medium.

$$MH_2 + NAD^+ / NADP^+ \rightarrow M + NADH + H^+ / NADPH + H^+$$
  • NAD-linked: Involved in oxidative/energy pathways (Glycolysis, TCA cycle, respiratory chain).
  • NADP-linked: Involved in reductive biosynthetic reactions (Fatty acid & cholesterol synthesis). Note: NADPH cannot be oxidized for energy production.

Flavin Coenzymes (FMN or FAD) Linked

Derived from Vitamin Riboflavin

Unlike NAD, both hydrogen atoms from the substrate are seamlessly accepted by FMN or FAD.

$$MH_2 + FAD / FMN \rightarrow M + FADH_2 / FMNH_2$$
  • FMN-linked: Mostly in mitochondrial electron transport chain (Ex: NADH-dehydrogenase).
  • FAD-linked: Ex: Succinate dehydrogenase (TCA cycle), Acyl-CoA dehydrogenase ($\beta$-oxidation of fatty acids).

3 Hydroperoxidases

These protective enzymes catalyze the reduction of harmful hydrogen peroxide ($H_2O_2$) into safe, life-giving water ($H_2O$).

  • Peroxidases
  • Catalases

4 Oxygenases

They catalyze the direct addition of oxygen atoms into the substrate. They are not involved in energy production.

Mono-oxygenases

Incorporates 1 O atom as a hydroxyl group; the other becomes $H_2O$.
Ex: Phenylalanine hydroxylase (forms Tyrosine).

Dioxygenases

Incorporates both O atoms into the substrate.
Ex: Homogentisate oxidase, Cycloxygenase (prostaglandin synthesis).

Part II: Quick Exam Revision

A lovely cheat-sheet for last-minute reading

High-Yield Short Notes

  • Core Concept: Biological oxidation is exergonic. Energy released $\rightarrow$ forms ATP (Oxidative Phosphorylation).
  • Enzyme Class: Oxidoreductases (4 sub-types: Oxidases, Dehydrogenases, Hydroperoxidases, Oxygenases).
  • 1. Oxidases: Uses $O_2$ as H-acceptor (forms $H_2O$/$H_2O_2$).
    Ex: Cytochrome oxidase, Xanthine oxidase.
  • 2. Dehydrogenases: Cannot use $O_2$. Requires Coenzymes.
    • NAD$^+$: (Niacin) 2H removed. 1 to NAD, 1 as $H^+$. For Energy (Glycolysis, TCA).
    • NADP$^+$: (Niacin) For Biosynthesis (Fatty acids). Cannot be oxidized for energy.
    • FMN/FAD: (Riboflavin) Both H accepted ($FADH_2$). Ex: Succinate dehydrogenase.
  • 3. Hydroperoxidases: Reduces $H_2O_2 \rightarrow H_2O$.
    Types: Catalases, Peroxidases.
  • 4. Oxygenases: Adds $O_2$ into substrate. No energy production.
    • Mono: Adds 1 O atom. Ex: Phenylalanine hydroxylase.
    • Di: Adds both O atoms. Ex: Cycloxygenase.
