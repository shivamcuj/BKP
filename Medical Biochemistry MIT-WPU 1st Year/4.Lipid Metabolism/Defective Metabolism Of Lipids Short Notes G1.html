Defective Metabolism of Lipids

A cozy study guide carefully crafted for your exams

In-Depth Study Notes

1. Atherosclerosis

Definition: A buildup of cholesterol plaque in the walls of arteries, which causes the narrowing of the arterial lumen and obstruction of blood flow. It is heavily associated with abnormal cholesterol metabolism.

The Pathogenesis (How it forms):

  • Initial Stage (Reversible): Cholesterol esters (specifically cholesterol oleates) from arterial smooth muscle cells deposit in the arterial intima. This creates fatty streaks. If controlled here, the condition can be reversed.
  • Plaque Formation: If uncontrolled, extracellular deposition continues along with apo B-100 lipoproteins. This solidifies into a plaque in the arterial wall.
  • Complications: The plaque promotes blood clot formation (thrombosis). If a clot forms in the coronary artery, oxygen supply to the heart diminishes.
  • Ultimate Result: Anoxia (lack of oxygen) causes necrosis of cardiac tissue, leading to Myocardial Infarction (heart attack), Stroke, and Coronary Artery Disease (CAD).
Important Note: Atherosclerosis can also develop as a secondary complication to diseases like diabetes, hypothyroidism, and lipid nephrosis. Sometimes, it happens even with normal cholesterol levels due to inflammatory factors and low HDL levels (which leads to monocyte infiltration and foam cell formation).

2. Fatty Liver

Definition: A normal liver contains about 5% lipid (one-quarter of which is triglyceride). When lipid accumulation becomes extensive, reaching 25% to 30%, it is known as a fatty liver. In severe cases, triglycerides can occupy the entire cytoplasm of the hepatocyte.

Primary Causes of Lipid Accumulation:

  • Raised Plasma Free Fatty Acids: Excessive fat mobilization from adipose tissues causes the liver to esterify more fatty acids into triglycerides. Because VLDL synthesis remains at a normal rate, the triglycerides pile up.
    Examples: Starvation, Diabetes, High-fat diet, Carnitine deficiency.
  • Metabolic Block in Lipoprotein (VLDL) Production: VLDL is required to transport triglycerides out of the liver. If VLDL formation is blocked, fats accumulate.
    The Cure: Lipotropic factors (Choline, Methionine, Betaine) prevent or cure this. For instance, choline deficiency blocks phospholipid biosynthesis, impairing the membranes needed to make VLDL.
  • Toxic Substances: Hepatotoxic agents damage the liver's ability to process fats.
    Examples: Carbon tetrachloride, chloroform, alcohol, arsenic, and lead. Drugs that inhibit protein synthesis (like puromycin) also cause this.

Medical Consequence: Chronic lipid accumulation leads to fibrotic changes in hepatocytes, progressing to cirrhosis and total impairment of liver function.

3. Hypercholesterolemia

Definition: An elevation in blood cholesterol levels greater than 200 mg/dl. While cholesterol is a vital natural metabolite (needed for cell membranes, steroid hormones, and bile acids), excess amounts lead to coronary heart disease.

Disorders causing Hypercholesterolemia:

  • Diabetes Mellitus: Increases cholesterol synthesis due to a high availability of Acetyl CoA.
  • Hypothyroidism (Myxoedema): Decreases the number of HDL receptors on hepatocytes, keeping cholesterol in the blood.
  • Obstructive Jaundice: Causes an obstruction in the excretion of cholesterol through bile.
  • Nephrotic Syndrome: Characterized by an increase in plasma globulin concentration and lipoprotein fractions.

Good vs. Bad Cholesterol

  • LDL (Bad Cholesterol): Lethally dangerous lipoprotein. It causes atherosclerosis. Small dense LDL (sdLDL) is the most dangerous fraction associated with Coronary Heart Disease.
  • HDL (Good Cholesterol): Highly desirable lipoprotein. High concentrations actively counteract atherogenesis (plaque formation).
Lifestyle Impacts: High blood pressure, emotional stress, smoking, drinking soft water, coffee drinking, lack of exercise, and abdominal obesity all significantly elevate serum cholesterol levels.

Last-Minute Revision Notes

Quick, punchy facts to glance at right before your exam.

Atherosclerosis

  • What: Cholesterol plaque buildup in arteries.
  • Start: Cholesterol oleates deposit -> Fatty streaks (Reversible).
  • End: Plaque + apo B-100 -> Luminal narrowing.
  • Result: Clots, Myocardial Infarction, Stroke, CAD.
  • Triggers: Low HDL, Diabetes, Hypothyroidism.

Fatty Liver

  • Normal vs Fatty: 5% lipid jumps to 25-30%.
  • Cause 1 (High FFA): Starvation, Diabetes. VLDL can't keep up with triglyceride synthesis.
  • Cause 2 (VLDL Block): Lack of Lipotropic factors (Choline, Methionine).
  • Cause 3 (Toxins): Alcohol, CCl4, Chloroform.
  • Endgame: Liver Cirrhosis.

Hypercholesterolemia

  • Level: Blood Cholesterol > 200 mg/dl.
  • Diabetes: High Acetyl CoA -> high synthesis.
  • Hypothyroidism: Low HDL receptors.
  • Jaundice: Blocked bile excretion.
  • Nephrotic Syndrome: High lipoproteins.

Cholesterol & Lifestyle

  • LDL (Bad): Causes plaque. sdLDL is the most dangerous.
  • HDL (Good): Counteracts plaque formation.
  • Risk Factors: Stress, smoking, obesity, coffee, lack of exercise, high BP.
