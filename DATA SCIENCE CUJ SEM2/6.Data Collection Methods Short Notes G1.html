1. Introduction & Basic Terms

Data Collection kya hai?
Ye ek systematic process hai jahan hum alag-alag sources se information (data) ikattha karte hain, measure karte hain aur analyze karte hain. Iska main goal hai kisi specific problem ki sahi picture clear karna. Har research ki shuruaat data collection se hi hoti hai.

Statistical Inquiry:
Simple words mein, ye 'sach ki khoj' (search for truth) hai using statistical methods. Ek investigator kisi topic pe jab deeply study karta hai aur facts/figures collect karta hai, toh use statistical inquiry kehte hain.

Important Terms Related to Data Collection

  • Data: Ye wo tool ya information hai jo humari problem solve karne me madad karta hai. (Types: Primary & Secondary).
  • Investigator: Wo main insaan jo statistical inquiry conduct kar raha hai (jo research kar raha hai).
  • Enumerators: Investigator ki help karne wale log jo ground par jaakar actual data collect karte hain.
  • Respondents: Wo aam log ya sources jinse data liya jata hai (jo questions ke answer dete hain).
  • Survey: Logon se unki pasand, napasand, product ka feedback lene ke method ko survey kehte hain.

2. Primary Data & Its Methods

Primary Data kya hai?
Ye wo data hota hai jo researcher ya investigator khud pheli baar (first-hand) collect karta hai apni specific research problem ke liye. Ye data bohot original aur accurate hota hai kyunki isme humein pura control hota hai.

Methods of Collecting Primary Data

1. Interviews

Direct one-on-one baat karke data lena.

  • Direct Personal: Investigator directly person se milta hai. (e.g., Housewives se directly milke routine poochna).
  • Indirect Oral: Direct person se na mil kar, usko janne walo se poochna. (e.g., Employee ka data uske boss se lena).

2. Questionnaires

Questions ka ek set ready karna.

  • Mailing Method: Questionnaire email/post se bhejna. (Sasta hai par log reply kam karte hain).
  • Enumerator Method: Enumerator khud form leke jata hai aur fill karwata hai.

3. Observations

Bina disturb kiye naturally kisi ka behavior observe karna. (Fayda: Authentic data. Nuksan: Observer bias ho sakta hai).

4. Experiments

Variables ko manipulate karke effect dekhna (cause-and-effect). (E.g., Nayi dawai (drug) test karna).

5. Focus Group

6-12 logo ko bula kar ek specific topic/product pe discussion karwana. Deep insights milte hain.

6. Local Sources / Correspondents

Alag-alag jagah par local agents ya correspondents rakhna jo wahan ka data bhejte rahein.

3. Secondary Data & Its Sources

Secondary Data kya hai?
Ye wo data hota hai jo kisi aur ne pehle se hi collect karke publish ya process kar diya hai. Humein bas wahan se lena hota hai. Ye sasta hota hai aur isme time bhi bohot kam lagta hai kyunki collection phase pehle hi koi aur kar chuka hai.

Sources of Secondary Data

Secondary data ke 2 main sources hote hain: Published aur Unpublished.

1. Published Sources

Jo data public ke liye available/print kar diya gaya ho.

  • Government Publications: Ministries ya Govt dwara chhapi gyi reports (e.g., Annual Survey of Industries). Ye kaafi reliable hote hain.
  • Semi-Govt Publications: Municipalities aadi ka data (Health, Birth/Death records).
  • Trade Associations: Industries ka apna data (e.g., Sugar Mills Association ka data).
  • Journals & Papers: Newspapers, Magazines me chhapa data.
  • International Publications: UNO, IMF, World Bank jaisi badi bodies ka data.
  • Research Institutions: Universities aur institutes ka published research.

2. Unpublished Sources

Jo data collect toh hua hai par public ke liye chapa nahi gaya hai.

Aksar organizations, professors, ya private companies apna data apne self-use ke liye collect karte hain, aur use kisi book ya website par publish nahi karte. Aise private/internal records ko Unpublished Secondary Data kehte hain.

4. Variables & Examples

Variable kya hota hai?
Koi bhi aisi quantity ya attribute jiski value time ke sath ya ek investigation se dusri investigation me change hoti rehti hai (vary karti hai), use Variable kehte hain.
Generally inko \( X \), \( Y \), ya \( Z \) jaise letters se represent kiya jata hai. Mathematical terms me, hum inhe equations me likh sakte hain, jaise \( Y = f(X) \).

Example: Production of Rice in India

Yahan neeche table me aap dekh sakte hain ki 'Year' (X) badalne par 'Production' (Y) bhi badal raha hai.

Year (X) Production of Rice in Million Tonnes (Y)
1950-1951 20.58
1966-1967 30.34
1975-1976 48.74
1998-1999 86.03
2020-2021 120.00
2022-2023 (Estimated) 135.52

Is table me 'Year' ek variable \( X \) hai, aur 'Rice Production' dusra variable \( Y \) hai. In dono ki values se hum India me rice production ka trend analyze kar sakte hain.

Exam-Ready Quick Revision

Comparison: Primary vs Secondary Data

Basis Primary Data Secondary Data
Meaning First-hand information khud collect ki hui. Dusre dwara collect ki hui pehle se available info.
Originality Highly original (Original Data). Not original (Kisi aur ka data hai).
Time & Cost Bohot expensive hai aur time zyada lagta hai. Sasta hai aur quickly mil jata hai.
Accuracy/Reliability Zyada accurate (researcher ke needs k hisaab se). Kam accurate ho sakta hai.
